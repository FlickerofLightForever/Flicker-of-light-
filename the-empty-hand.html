The Empty Hand

But why?

A question that remained even after everything else had turned to ashes.

All he had ever asked for was someone to love him and hold his empty hand.

It didn't seem like too much to ask.

At least, not when he was younger.

He never wanted a perfect person or an extraordinary life. He wanted someone to share the ordinary things with. Someone to laugh with over nothing, to talk to at the end of a long day, and to sit beside when there were no words left to say.

He wanted to feel that someone was genuinely there.

But life rarely followed the path people imagined. People came into his life, some stayed, and others left. He didn't always understand why. Some encounters became memories, while others faded before he even realised they had happened.

He wasn't always right about people either. Sometimes he trusted too quickly, misunderstood a silence, or spoke before thinking. He could be stubborn, make mistakes, and get things wrong. But he learned along the way. He learned that understanding someone didn't mean agreeing with everything they did, and that forgiving someone didn't always mean asking them to stay.

Still, he never wanted to become someone who hesitated before offering a hand. If he saw someone struggling, he often reached out before considering the consequences. Sometimes his heart simply moved faster than his head.

He had difficult days of his own. There were people who tried to comfort him, and he appreciated them. But sometimes he didn't need advice or someone to tell him what to do. He just wanted someone to listen, to sit beside him, and to understand what he couldn't always put into words.

Perhaps that was why he found comfort in writing.

On a page, he could take his time. He could follow a thought without being interrupted, return to a memory, or give words to a feeling he couldn't quite explain. Sometimes he wrote about things he had experienced. Other times, his imagination took him somewhere he had never been.

He found stories in ordinary moments. A passing conversation, a familiar place, a smile, or something left unsaid could stay with him long after everyone else had moved on. Some people became part of his stories without ever knowing it. He remembered where those stories began, even when the people themselves had long forgotten the moment.

He never knew exactly where a thought would lead when he started writing. Sometimes the words came easily; other times, he had to sit with them for days. He simply followed them until they became something he could share.

Perhaps there were things he had wanted to say for years but had never found the right person to hear. So he wrote them instead.

And he kept living. Meeting people, discovering things, making mistakes, and finding reasons to look forward to another day. There were still places he hadn't seen, people he hadn't met, and stories he hadn't imagined.

Perhaps his story would remain unfinished, even at the end.

There would still be words he hadn't written, memories he hadn't understood, and questions he had carried for a lifetime.

And perhaps, when there was nothing left to write, he would still find himself holding on to one last thought.

A moment that existed only in his imagination.

Something that might bring a smile to his face, even after everything else had turned to ashes.

But why?

Was it all worth it? Or was that final smile simply the last beautiful thing his imagination had allowed him to believe?
