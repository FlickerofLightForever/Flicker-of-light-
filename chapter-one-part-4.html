Chapter One · Part 4

Let Me See You One More Time

The First Memory · A story told one memory at a time

He smiled and closed his eyes. A gentle breeze brushed against his face before quietly moving on, leaving behind a strange sense of relief. It felt as though everything the sky had kept hidden was finally raining down upon him. He slowly raised his arm toward the sky, as if asking, Are you looking at the same thing I am? Behind the clouds, the sun began to emerge, spilling its warmth over him. For a moment, it seemed as though the sky had heard his unspoken question. I'm here, it seemed to say. And I have a story to tell you—a story that will last longer than the world itself. Perhaps it was only his imagination. Perhaps the sky had said nothing at all. But inside his mind, a story was already beginning to unfold, and he was too lost in it to notice anything else. Not even the fact that he was supposed to be at school. The memories of the previous night had followed him into the morning, filling his thoughts until he forgot the time, his plans, and the world waiting beyond his imagination. Yet somehow, his feet remembered the way. Once again, they carried him down the same road. He walked along it, turned back, and wandered as though he had lost something there. Perhaps he had. Only he didn't know what it was, or how to search for something he couldn't even name. Somehow, the road had become familiar to him, a place he found himself returning to without quite knowing why. But life had other plans. As the boy wandered along the road, one of his teachers happened to be walking to school along the same path. Recognizing him, the teacher stopped and asked what he was doing there. The boy had no easy explanation. Before he could find one, the teacher asked him to come along. And so, reluctantly, he left the road behind, walking beside the very person who had unknowingly interrupted his little search. Or at least, that was what he thought. --- The girl returned to the place where their eyes had first met. She told herself she would stay for only a little while. Perhaps he would pass by again. Perhaps he wouldn't. Either way, she would soon have to leave. The road carried on with its ordinary life. People came and went, strangers passed one another, and the morning slowly moved toward afternoon. Different faces passed before her, each carrying a story of their own, each moving along with the rhythm of life. And somewhere among them, she was searching for one particular face. She knew nothing about him, not even his name. He was just another stranger passing through the world, like all the others. Yet she kept wondering whether she would see that smile again. Had he smiled because he liked her? Or had she done something that made him smile? She didn't know. Perhaps that was what made her so curious. After a while, she decided she had waited long enough. But as she began to walk away, she found herself looking back at the road. There was no reason to stay. She didn't know his name, where he lived, or whether he even remembered her. They had shared nothing more than a brief moment and a smile. Still, she couldn't quite bring herself to leave. Just a little longer, she told herself. --- Back at school, the boy tried to return to the world he was supposed to belong to. The lessons continued, voices filled the classroom, and the hours moved forward. Yet his thoughts kept drifting back to the same memory. A smile. A wave. A face he barely knew. They hadn't even introduced themselves. He knew nothing about her. There was no name to remember, no promise to keep, no reason to believe he would ever see her again. Still, he kept wondering what might happen if he did. Would she smile at him? Would she wave? He didn't know why those little things mattered so much. He only knew that he wanted to experience them once more. As the morning slipped into afternoon, the classroom began to feel distant. Then the rain started tapping against the window. He glanced outside. Perhaps she would be there today. He had no reason to believe she would return. She didn't know him any more than he knew her. But the possibility was enough. Before he could talk himself out of it, he slipped away from class and hurried outside. He simply wanted to see her one more time. --- By the time the afternoon had begun to fade, the girl was still there. The hours had passed almost unnoticed. The faces had changed, the road had grown quieter, and the morning she had arrived in was now only a distant memory. She glanced down the road once more. Perhaps he wouldn't come. Perhaps she had mistaken a simple smile for something worth waiting for. The thought made her smile faintly at herself. Still, she wasn't quite ready to leave. The first drops of rain touched her skin. She looked up at the sky. The road had begun to empty, and the rain was growing heavier, darkening the ground beneath her feet. At last, she lowered her gaze and started walking away. She had barely gone a few steps when a familiar voice called from behind. ‘There you are! I've been looking everywhere for you.’ It was her friend. ‘Come on,’ her friend said, glancing at the darkening sky. ‘Let's go home before this rain gets any worse.’ The girl nodded, but before following, she looked back at the road one last time. The road was empty. There was no sign of him. She turned away, and the two began walking home. --- The boy hurried down the familiar road, his eyes searching the places where he imagined he might see her. The rain fell harder, blurring the world around him. He barely noticed. His thoughts were already somewhere ahead, caught between hope and a thousand little questions he couldn't answer. Then his foot slipped. He lost his balance, reaching out instinctively to catch himself, but there was nothing to hold on to. Before he could recover, he hit the wet road hard. Pain rushed through him. Someone shouted. Footsteps hurried toward him. A stranger knelt beside him while another called for an ambulance. Yet the boy barely seemed to notice the commotion around him. His eyes remained open, fixed on the grey sky above. Rain slipped across his face, tracing the paths where tears might have fallen. But he wasn't crying. Perhaps the pain was there, but his thoughts were still somewhere else, searching for something he hadn't found. He had come so close to something he couldn't even name. Looking up at the grey sky, he couldn't help but wonder why things never seemed to happen the way he wished. Why was something that had brought him so much happiness suddenly so far beyond his reach? He wanted to ask the sky, but no words came. Only the rain answered, falling quietly against his skin. --- The girl and her friend had barely begun walking when a distant siren cut through the sound of the rain. An ambulance. The girl slowed and looked toward the sound. ‘What happened?’ she asked. ‘How would I know?’ her friend replied. ‘Come on. We should go home.’ But the siren held her attention. She couldn't explain why, only that something inside her urged her to see where it was going. ‘Can we take the other road?’ she asked. Her friend stared at her. ‘That's the longer way.’ ‘Please?’ Her friend studied her face for a moment, then sighed. ‘All right. But we're going straight home afterward.’ They turned toward the siren. By the time they reached the road, a small crowd had gathered around an injured boy. Paramedics knelt beside him, working quickly while rain poured over the scene. People stood close together, blocking the view. The girl slowed as she passed, searching through the crowd, but the rain and the shifting figures kept the scene beyond her clear view. For a moment, she lingered. Then her friend gently tugged her arm. ‘Come on.’ She hesitated before following. Behind them, the paramedics continued their work. Then they carefully lifted the boy onto a stretcher and carried him toward the ambulance. The sky slipped from his view. For one last moment, a narrow strip of grey remained above him. Then the doors closed. The rain was shut out. The road disappeared. The world beyond the ambulance became a distant blur. --- Through the rain, their paths crossed without them knowing. They came so close, close enough to touch, yet still remained apart. The rain saw how close they were, but their eyes never met. And the rain continued to fall.
