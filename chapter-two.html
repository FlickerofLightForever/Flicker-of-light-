The Connected Story

Chapter Two — The First Memory

Part One — A Smile Beneath the Sun

Chapter Two — Part One For a while, he thought the past stayed where he left it. He had learned not to look back too often. Not because there was nothing there, but because some memories had a way of making the present feel smaller. That night, he was sitting alone beside a window, watching rain gather on the glass. There was nothing unusual about the evening. The room was quiet. A cup of coffee had gone cold beside him. The light above the table had been left on, though he had stopped reading some time ago. He was simply sitting there, listening to the rain. Then, somewhere between one drop and the next, he remembered the smell of wet earth. He closed his eyes. For a moment, the room disappeared. He was younger again. Not by much. But enough to remember a version of himself who had not yet learned to be careful with hope. Back then, his days were ordinary. He woke up, went where he needed to go, came home when the streets began to quiet, and filled the spaces in between with whatever kept his mind occupied. He had people around him. He laughed when something was funny. He answered when someone spoke to him. He knew how to sit in a room full of people without looking lonely. Still, there were evenings when he would walk home alone and wonder what it would feel like to have someone waiting for him. Not someone who owed him anything. Not someone who had promised to stay. Just someone who would notice when he was gone. It was a small wish. At least, he thought it was. He never spoke about it. Some wishes sound foolish when spoken aloud. So he kept his to himself and continued living as though he had never made it. Until one afternoon, on a day he would later remember far more clearly than he had any reason to, he took a different route home. He didn't know why. There was no particular reason for it. He simply turned down a street he normally passed without noticing. The rain had stopped not long before. Water still rested along the edge of the pavement, reflecting pieces of the grey sky. He walked slowly, watching his steps. And then he saw her. Not in the way stories usually describe such moments. There was no music. No sudden feeling that the world had stopped. He almost walked past her. She was standing a little distance away, looking at something across the street, completely unaware that anyone was watching her. For a few seconds, he simply stood there. Then she looked up. Their eyes met. Only for a moment. She smiled. It was small. Almost absent-minded. And then she looked away. He kept walking. That should have been the end of it. At the time, neither of them knew that it wasn't. He reached the end of the street before he realized he was smiling. He looked down at himself, almost embarrassed by it. There was nothing to smile about. He didn't know her. Didn't know her name. Didn't know where she lived, where she was going, or whether he would ever see her again. Yet somehow, the walk home felt different. Years later, he would wonder if that was how it began. Not with love. Not even with hope. Just with a smile from a stranger on a wet afternoon— and a man who had no idea how much of his life had just passed him on the street. The following morning, he went about his day as usual. At least, that was what he told himself. Nothing had changed. The same streets. The same morning noise. The same small routines he had never thought twice about. And yet, somewhere between getting dressed and stepping out into the morning, he remembered her smile. He couldn't explain why. He had seen it for barely a second. Still, it had stayed. He tried not to make anything of it. There was nothing to make of. She was a stranger. He didn't even know if he would see her again. So he let the thought go and continued with his day. Until later that afternoon, when he found himself standing at the same corner again. He hadn't planned to take that road. At least, that's what he told himself. He had almost reached the corner when a voice called from somewhere behind him. Something inside him made him stop. It was strange. He had never heard that voice before, yet for some reason, it felt as though some quiet part of him had been waiting for it. He turned. She was there. She was talking with a friend, standing where a narrow piece of sunlight had slipped between the buildings and found her. The light touched only a small part of her, almost as if the sun had chosen that one place to rest. For a moment, she seemed to glow. Like a kiss from the sun. He stood there longer than he meant to. Then she looked up. Their eyes met. The second time. But somehow, it didn't feel like the first. Maybe because yesterday she had only been a stranger who smiled at him. Today, there was something familiar in that smile. She smiled again. And without thinking, he smiled back. Neither of them said anything. They didn't need to. For that brief moment, with the sunlight resting against her and the rest of the world moving around them, he simply stood there and looked at her. He didn't know it yet, but that was the moment the stranger from yesterday began becoming someone his memory would refuse to forget. He wasn't sure why he did it. Maybe because he had seen her yesterday. Maybe because he had seen her again today. Maybe he simply wanted to know what would happen if, for once, he stopped pretending he hadn't noticed her. So he lifted his hand. “Hi.” She looked at him for a moment, then raised her hand and waved back. She was still surrounded by her friends, still caught in whatever conversation they were having, yet somehow that small wave felt like it had been meant for him alone. It was such a small thing. Barely anything. Yet he watched it the way morning light catches a single drop of dew on a leaf—small enough to disappear without anyone noticing, but bright enough to make you stop and look. He smiled. He still didn't know her name. But somehow, that little wave stayed with him long after he had walked away. She had already turned back to her friend, continuing their conversation as though nothing had happened. He wanted to say something more. Maybe he could have. But he didn't. A simple wave was all he had managed. He walked away smiling to himself, almost embarrassed, as though he had just done something funny. Yet he was happy. He didn't know why. Nothing had really happened. He had only said hello. She had only waved back. And still, somewhere inside him, something felt different. Before he reached home, he passed a couple standing by the side of the street, talking quietly to each other. He glanced at them and suddenly felt strangely shy. He didn't know why. Maybe it was the way they stood together. Maybe it was simply seeing two people who had somewhere to be with each other. Whatever it was, his mind betrayed him. For a moment, he wasn't looking at them anymore. He was looking at her. That same face. That small piece of sunlight resting against her skin. The way she had seemed to glow beneath it. Like the sun had leaned down and kissed her for a moment. He looked away, almost embarrassed by a memory that wasn't even supposed to be there. She wasn't beside him. She wasn't even on the same street anymore. And somehow, he was still carrying her home. That night was harder than he expected. He lay awake for a while before finally getting up and walking to the window. The city had grown quiet. He looked up at the sky. The moon was there, bright and distant above the rooftops. He stared at it for a long time. Then, almost without thinking, he wondered if she was looking at it too. He smiled at the thought. He didn't know her name. He didn't know where she lived. He didn't even know if he would see her again tomorrow. And yet, for the first time in his life, the moon didn't feel so far away. — The memory had only just begun. —
